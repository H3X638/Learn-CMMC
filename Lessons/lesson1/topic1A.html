Topic 1A: CMMC Foundations

Understand the origins, purpose, and structure of the Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC).

15–30 minutes 10 knowledge points

The Origins of CMMC

The Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) was created by the U.S. Department of Defense to protect Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) handled by contractors in the Defense Industrial Base (DIB). Before CMMC, contractors were required to follow NIST SP 800-171, which outlines 110 cybersecurity controls.

Compliance was self-attested, meaning companies could claim they met requirements without verification. Over time, repeated data breaches showed this approach was ineffective. To address this, the DoD introduced CMMC to ensure all contractors implement and verify strong cybersecurity practices.

Why CMMC Matters

CMMC ensures that every organization handling DoD information protects it to a verified standard. Certification is now a requirement for winning certain contracts, making it a business necessity rather than an optional security goal.

  • Identifies and corrects cybersecurity weaknesses
  • Encourages consistent, repeatable practices
  • Builds trust throughout the defense supply chain

The Evolution of the Model

CMMC version 1.0 was released in 2020 and included five levels of maturity. Feedback from industry led to the release of CMMC 2.0 in 2021, simplifying the model into three levels aligned with NIST SP 800-171.

  • Level 1 – Foundational: Protects Federal Contract Information (FCI) with 17 basic practices. Verification is self-assessed annually.
  • Level 2 – Advanced: Protects Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) using 110 practices from NIST SP 800-171. Some programs require third-party assessments every three years.
  • Level 3 – Expert: Protects against advanced persistent threats (APTs) using NIST SP 800-172. Verification is performed by the government.

Practices and Processes

CMMC focuses on two key elements: practices and processes. Practices are specific security actions, such as enabling multi-factor authentication or encrypting data at rest. Processes describe how these practices are maintained over time, ensuring cybersecurity becomes part of day-to-day operations.

Benefits Beyond Compliance

CMMC drives long-term resilience. Implementing its practices improves protection against breaches, strengthens incident response, and aligns organizations with frameworks like ISO 27001 and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework. Certified companies often find compliance boosts reputation and competitiveness.
